# Home emergency drill record generator

An emergency plan can look complete on paper while depending on a blocked exit, a contact nobody can reach, an item only one person can find or a support role that was never accepted. A calm, announced household exercise can expose those gaps before a real event. This free generator turns what the household actually practiced into a dated working record: the plan or official guidance used, participants, observations by phase, support checks, communication and reunion checks, status counts, improvements and a repeat date.

The result is not a building inspection, fire-code approval, evacuation order, medical plan or safety certificate. It does not choose a route during a real hazard. Local authorities, emergency services, building management and current conditions remain the sources for real-world action. The generator has a narrower job: preserve an honest account of an exercise and make unfinished work visible.

## Begin with the plan and authority, not the timer

Before entering a duration, identify the current source used to design the exercise. That may be a local emergency-management page, fire-department material, a building evacuation plan, a school or care plan, or a household plan already derived from those sources. Include a version or review date. “Internet checklist” is not retrievable enough; “City emergency page reviewed August 23, household plan v3” lets the family verify whether the source has changed.

For home fire escape planning, the U.S. Fire Administration's current [home fire escape plan guidance](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/home-fire-escape-plans/index.html) emphasizes mapping doors and windows, identifying more than one way out of rooms, keeping routes unobstructed, choosing an outside meeting place and practicing with everyone in the home. Its high-rise guidance separately points residents toward exit stairs and building-specific procedures. Those messages are useful inputs to a fire exercise, but they do not make a generic web tool the authority for every property.

An earthquake, flood, wildfire, storm or hazardous-material exercise can require different decisions. Do not reuse fire instructions just because the form has an “evacuation” label. Select the exercise type, then write the actual authority and plan version that apply where the household lives. If local guidance and an old household note disagree, update the household note rather than treating it as permanent.

## Announce the exercise and define what is simulated

A useful household drill does not require a surprise. Unexpected alarms, smoke, darkness, blocked exits or disabled equipment can frighten children, alarm neighbors, create falls, interfere with accessibility equipment or trigger an unnecessary emergency response. State the goal and boundaries before starting. For example: walk the planned paths, meet at the household's agreed location reference and test a backup contact; do not produce smoke, lock a door, disconnect power, use an elevator contrary to building rules or physically carry someone without an agreed safe method.

The scope field preserves that agreement. It distinguishes a low-risk walk-through from an actual incident and makes a stopped exercise informative rather than embarrassing. If a participant feels unsafe, if conditions change, if an exit is not suitable to test, or if the building asks the household to stop, select `Stopped for safety` and record the follow-up. A stopped row is evidence that the record is honest.

## Choose one exercise purpose at a time

The generator offers several purposes because “emergency drill” is too broad to be a useful record on its own:

- A whole-home evacuation walk-through checks whether the household can locate and discuss the planned exits, meeting reference and roles.
- Home fire escape practice should be tied to current fire-service and building guidance, including the abilities of everyone in the home.
- An earthquake response and post-shaking assembly review separates protective actions during shaking from later movement and reunion decisions.
- A communication and reunion exercise tests how household members report status when separated without publishing their precise routines.
- An accessibility, caregiver and pet support check tests whether both primary and backup roles understand the agreed plan without copying medical files into the drill sheet.

Combining every hazard, every route, every contact and every dependent into one long exercise usually produces vague observations. A shorter exercise with a clear purpose is easier to repeat after corrections. If the household needs to test a second scenario, create a separate record with its own source, date and boundaries.

## Record participants as context, not signatures

Participants may be names inside a private household file or simply roles such as Adult 1, child participant, overnight guest, caregiver backup or pet support role. The generator accepts one to twelve unique entries. It does not verify identity, training, consent, legal authority or attendance. A person appearing in the list is context for the exercise, not proof that they accepted a responsibility.

Before the practice, ask each person to confirm the part they are expected to try. Children, older adults, disabled household members, people who use mobility or communication supports, caregivers and guests may experience the same plan differently. The U.S. Fire Administration's [fire-safety information for people with disabilities](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/at-risk-audiences/people-with-disabilities/) reinforces that escape planning needs to reflect a person's actual abilities. A record that says “everyone can use the same route” without testing or consultation can hide the most important gap.

## Five columns turn a memory into an observation

Each exercise observation uses five pipe-separated columns:

1. Phase
2. Planned check
3. Observed result
4. Improvement and owner
5. Status

The phase should identify a distinct part of the exercise, such as start and awareness, route walk-through, exit access, meeting and count, offline information lookup or return and debrief. Each phase appears once, so related notes stay together.

The planned check states what the household intended to learn. The observed result records only what happened. “The alternate path was not tested because the window was difficult to operate” is more valuable than “alternate route OK.” “Backup contact saw the test message after seven minutes” is more precise than “communication works.” An exercise is not improved by writing a better result than the participants actually observed.

The improvement column names both an action and an owner. “Review exits” is vague. “Adult 2: confirm the alternate option with the building plan before the repeat exercise” can be closed. If a change requires a landlord, building manager, fire department, accessibility specialist or other qualified source, name the contact step without inventing the solution.

## Four statuses prevent false completion

`Observed as planned` means the specific check produced the result described in the current household plan. It does not mean the property is certified safe. `Needs follow-up` means an observed gap has an owner. `Not tested` preserves something that remained outside the exercise. `Stopped for safety` records that continuing was not appropriate.

The generated status count is deliberately not a score. Three rows observed as planned and one row not tested do not equal “75% safe.” Hazards differ in importance, and a single blocked or unsuitable route may matter more than several easy checks. Use the count to find unfinished work, not to compare households or pressure participants.

The duration field follows the same rule. Record one to 240 whole minutes as an observation. Do not present it as a universal pass mark. Some official fire campaigns use time-based practice to communicate how quickly a fire can become dangerous, but a household's exercise time can be affected by the scenario, dwelling, accessibility needs, age of participants and what was intentionally simulated. The authoritative plan—not the generator—defines the relevant practice method.

## Test support roles without copying private medical details

The support section has four columns: person, pet or support scenario; primary role; backup role; and observed result. It asks whether the agreed support can be found and understood, not why a person needs it. “Mobility support scenario | Adult 1 | Neighbor previously confirmed as backup | transfer method was not tested; professional plan needs review” can be useful. A diagnosis, complete medication list, dosage, identity number or detailed care history does not belong in the shareable drill record.

Link the row to a protected care plan maintained by the appropriate person and professional source. If a medical device, oxygen system, powered mobility aid or other life-supporting equipment is involved, do not improvise a shutdown, movement or backup-power procedure for the sake of a web form. Record that the item was excluded and assign the authoritative review.

For pets, test household logistics rather than animal medicine. Can the carrier, leash or transport item be found without a secret code? Does a primary and backup person know which animal they cover? Is the veterinary or care plan available from a protected location? Do not generate feeding amounts, medication doses or emergency treatment instructions here.

## Separate communication from precise location disclosure

The communication and reunion table records a scenario, primary method or meeting reference, backup and observed result. A household may test a group text, an out-of-area contact or a protected offline card. It can refer to “primary meeting point” and “alternate meeting point in protected plan” instead of printing a vulnerable household member's schedule or exact location on a copy that may be shared.

Testing a contact means using an announced test message and recording whether the intended person acknowledged it. It does not guarantee that networks will work during a disaster. Meeting places, shelters, roads and local instructions can change with the hazard. During a real event, use current alerts and authority guidance; do not follow a stale drill sheet into an unsafe area.

## A worked record that keeps an untested item visible

Suppose a household runs an announced whole-home walk-through on August 23 using its current city guidance and building plan. The primary path is clear, but the household does not test an alternate window because it is hard to operate and the safe method is uncertain. A truthful row is:

`Exit walk-through | Planned exits can be reached without moving stored items | Primary path clear; alternate window not tested | Adult 2: ask building management and local fire service which alternate option applies | Not tested`

This row does not call the window a usable exit, and it does not ask anyone to climb through it. The owner first gets authoritative information. After an appropriate correction, the household creates a new dated exercise record. The original remains unchanged so the history shows what improved.

A communication row might read:

`Household separated locally | Family group text | Out-of-area contact | Test message acknowledged by the intended participants`

That result is limited to the announced test. The next debrief can ask whether everyone recognized the message, whether the offline contact card was available and whether a newly added family member was included.

## Renters and multi-unit residents need building context

An apartment or condominium household does not control every exit, alarm, corridor or assembly procedure. Do not block common areas, trigger a building alarm, use equipment or conduct a group exercise that conflicts with management or local rules. Review posted instructions and ask the responsible building contact how household practice should fit the building plan.

Record shared-area problems through the proper channel instead of silently “fixing” safety equipment. A drill row can state that a stairwell instruction was unclear and assign a management inquiry. It cannot declare the building compliant or noncompliant. Formal inspections and enforcement belong to the responsible authorities and qualified professionals.

## Close the loop instead of filing the sheet

After the debrief, assign every `Needs follow-up`, `Not tested` and `Stopped for safety` row. Store the exercise record under a versioned label, preserve any approved plan or official source reference, and set a repeat date later than the exercise date. A repeat record should point back to the specific correction it is checking.

Update the household's real source records after a successful correction: the emergency contact organizer, protected document index, household plan, caregiver agreement or pet handoff. Do not leave the only correct answer inside a one-time drill export. The record proves that the household learned something only when the underlying plan changes and the revised step is practiced again.

## Privacy and real-emergency boundary

The generator runs in the current browser. FamilyBoard does not receive the entries. Copying, downloading, printing or saving to the local app creates copies under your control. Before sharing, remove full addresses, access codes, alarm codes, financial identifiers, diagnoses, medication doses and precise routines that are not needed by the recipient. The tool checks for some common sensitive labels, but no text filter can identify every secret.

In a real emergency, stop using the generator. Follow current official alerts, contact the emergency service for the place you are in, and respond to the actual conditions. A previous exercise cannot prove that a route, shelter, device, contact or building system is available now. This page helps a household learn from practice; it does not replace emergency judgment or professional planning.
