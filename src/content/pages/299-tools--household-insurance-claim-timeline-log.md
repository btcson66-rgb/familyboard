# Household Insurance Claim Timeline Log

After a leak, storm, fire, theft or accidental damage, a household may have several clocks running at once. Someone observes a condition. A policy or insurer source is located. A notice is sent or received. The insurer asks for documents. A qualified person may inspect the property. A coverage or claim decision may arrive later. A repair, replacement or complaint can create another follow-up. These are related events, but they are not interchangeable evidence.

The free Insurance Claim Timeline Log gives each event a dated row and a safe pointer. It answers “what did we observe, when, and which source did we check?” without copying a policy, claim number, address, estimate, photograph, private message, medical detail or payment information into a shared household list. It organises your records; it does not open a claim or predict an outcome.

## Record an incident without turning observation into a conclusion

Choose a short incident or asset code that means something only inside your protected record system. In the scope field, write what was actually observable and any temporary boundary, such as “visible ceiling mark; no cause conclusion” or “affected cupboard contents isolated for qualified review.” Do not turn an observation into a diagnosis, coverage opinion, safety conclusion or accusation.

The event date is the day the observation was made, not an assumed date of damage. A source pointer can identify a protected photo set, dated policy version, insurer notice, public regulator page or document folder without reproducing its contents. Distinguish an acknowledgement, document request, inspection appointment, document delivery, decision, payment observation and complaint route. “The insurer received the notice” is not the same as “the claim was accepted.”

## Five passes for a clear claim record

1. **Stabilise and observe.** Follow emergency and professional instructions first. Once safe, write only what the household can directly observe and point to protected evidence. This is not a safety checklist.
2. **Map controlling sources.** Identify the current issued policy version, insurer claim process, and any applicable regulator or ombudsman. Keep actual documents protected and record only safe pointers.
3. **Separate notice from decision.** Record when a notice, acknowledgement, request, inspection or decision was observed. A submitted form is not a confirmed settlement.
4. **Track document handoffs.** Record a protected bundle pointer, responsible role and date when documents are prepared or delivered. Do not paste estimates, receipts, personal details or correspondence into the shared timeline.
5. **Review the next unresolved question.** Ask whether the next fact must come from the insurer, regulator, contractor, adjuster or qualified adviser. Create a new version when scope or source changes so the earlier observation remains intact.

## How it connects to other FamilyBoard records

A home inventory describes items and their source records. A repair log describes service work and observed changes. An insurance claim timeline connects an incident to policy and insurer-source events without asserting that the incident is covered or that a payment is owed. An incident row can point to an inventory version and a repair-evidence timeline, while each page remains responsible for its own evidence and limitations.

## Privacy, safety and professional boundaries

Use a private code rather than a full address, policy number, claim number, telephone number, email address, person name, payment detail, health information or private correspondence. The browser tool does not contact an insurer, upload photographs, inspect a policy portal, authenticate a claim, calculate a deadline, interpret exclusions, estimate a benefit, determine liability or provide financial, insurance or legal advice. Emergencies, safety issues, coverage questions and disputes belong with the relevant emergency service, insurer, regulator, ombudsman and qualified professional.
