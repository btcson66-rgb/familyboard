# Move-out condition report generator

A useful move-out report does more than say that a room was “fine” or “damaged.” It identifies when the inspection happened, what the observer could actually see, which original file supports that observation, whether the parties agree, and who owns the next action. This free generator creates that structured working record for rooms, keys or access items, and meters or services. It runs in your browser and does not send the entries to FamilyBoard.

The result is intentionally not a lease addendum, signature, invoice, deposit statement or legal notice. Housing rules and required forms vary sharply by location. Use the output to improve the quality of your evidence and handover conversation, then complete the official process required by the actual lease and jurisdiction.

## What the generator records

The first four fields establish context: a premises label, the stage of the record, the date you actually inspected, and the planned or completed handover date. Use a short private label such as “Maple apartment” rather than putting a full address into something you may later copy or print. The stage distinguishes a tenant’s pre-move self-check from a joint handover and a post-handover follow-up. That distinction matters because an item seen by one person before the appointment is not automatically a finding accepted by everyone.

The agreement and starting-condition field points back to the material used for comparison. It might identify the lease, a signed check-in inventory, a dated move-in photo set, an amendment or a prior repair record. Write a reference that lets you reopen the exact version. “Move-in photos” is weak if three similar folders exist; “2025-09-01 check-in photos, original export” is much easier to retrieve.

Participants are recorded as names or roles for context, not as signatures. A joint walkthrough might list “Tenant” and “Property manager.” If another person attends as a witness or contractor, describe the role accurately. The generator accepts up to eight entries and removes duplicates, but it does not confirm anyone’s identity or authority.

## Write an observation, not a verdict

Each condition row has five required columns separated by a vertical bar:

1. Area or room
2. Observed condition
3. Evidence reference
4. Next action and owner
5. Status

An observation should describe what was visible or what occurred during an agreed test. “Dark mark about 4 cm wide at the lower right of the bedroom wall” is more useful than “tenant damage.” “Sink cabinet dry during the joint check” is narrower and more defensible than “plumbing perfect.” Cause, responsibility, repair scope and cost may require comparison with the starting record, contract language, normal-wear rules, professional diagnosis or a legal process. The tool does not infer those conclusions.

The evidence reference should lead to an original image, video segment, email or document. A practical file pattern is `YYYY-MM-DD_area_sequence`, followed by a short neutral note in your private index. Photograph the wider room first so the location is recognizable, then take closer images of the specific condition. Preserve the originals and avoid relying only on screenshots sent through a messaging app, which may be compressed or lose useful context.

The next-action column combines the task and its owner. Examples include “Tenant: remove remaining shelf items by handover,” “Manager: compare with check-in image 019,” or “Both: add the agreed wording to the official inspection form.” If no further action is currently recorded, say that; do not leave an ambiguous blank that looks like the row was forgotten.

## Four statuses that preserve uncertainty

`Open` means work or documentation is still needed. `Ready to confirm` means the row has enough information for the next shared review but is not yet jointly accepted. `Disputed` keeps a material difference visible. `Confirmed` should be used only when the intended participants actually confirmed the row in the process they are using.

Those statuses describe workflow, not legal effect. A row marked Confirmed in this browser output is still not a signature or waiver. Conversely, selecting Disputed does not prove that either side is correct. It simply prevents the record from silently converting an unresolved point into false consensus.

The result includes a count by status. That makes the report operational: before the handover ends, you can see whether any Open or Disputed rows still need an owner, another file or a written follow-up. A clean count is helpful, but accuracy matters more than forcing every row to Confirmed.

## Count keys and access items separately

Keys, access cards, remotes, parking devices and other physical access items need their own table. Enter one row per item with four columns: item, whole-number count, recipient or return status, and evidence reference. The count must be between zero and 99. If a property truly has no such item, a zero-count “Not applicable” row is clearer than leaving the section blank.

Do not type an alarm code, smart-lock code, lockbox PIN or password into this tool. A handover record can say that a credential was changed through a protected process without exposing the credential itself. The generator checks the shareable fields for common secret labels and asks you to replace them with a protected-location reference.

## Capture readings without pretending to settle the bill

The meter or service table accepts a service, an observed reading or status, its unit, and an evidence reference. Electricity and water are common examples, but the correct list depends on the property and provider. Use the unit shown on the meter or bill rather than assuming one. If a meter was inaccessible or the account is managed centrally, record that observed limitation and identify the document or contact needed next.

A photographed reading is not the same thing as a final bill, account transfer or proof that every charge has been paid. Providers may use different cut-off times, billing cycles, estimates and account processes. Complete the provider’s official close or transfer steps and keep the confirmation separately.

## Date rules prevent a false inspection history

The inspection field accepts only today or an earlier date because it represents an event that actually occurred. Future activity belongs in the planned handover and follow-up fields. The handover cannot be earlier than the inspection, and the next follow-up cannot be earlier than the handover. If a joint appointment has not happened yet, select the pre-move stage, enter the real self-check date, and keep future dates clearly described as planned.

This separation reduces a common records problem: a checklist created in advance can later look like evidence that the inspection was completed. The generated heading preserves the stage and dates so a reader can tell what the record actually represents.

## A worked example

Suppose the pre-move self-check happens on August 23 and the joint handover is planned for August 28. The tenant compares the apartment against a dated check-in inventory. In the bedroom, an old wall mark appears in both the move-in and current photo sets, but the property manager has not reviewed the comparison. A useful row is:

`Bedroom | Existing lower-wall mark still visible | Current IMG_004 and check-in IMG_019 | Manager: compare both originals at handover | Ready to confirm`

That row says what is visible, where the evidence lives, who needs to act and what the workflow status is. It does not assert who caused the mark or what it should cost. If the participants reach different conclusions at the handover, change the status to Disputed and preserve both positions in the official record rather than deleting one set of evidence.

## Use the report during a real walkthrough

Start with a wide image of each room and then move systematically through walls, ceilings, floors, doors, windows, fixed fixtures and included appliances. Record only what you can observe safely. Do not dismantle equipment or perform hazardous testing to complete a row. For any item that needs specialist diagnosis, record the symptom and the next contact rather than inventing a cause.

At the joint appointment, compare each row with the starting-condition material and the actual property. Count access items in view of the receiving party when practical. Capture meter readings close enough to the relevant handover time to make the record meaningful. Add unresolved items immediately, with a named owner and follow-up date, so they do not disappear into separate text messages.

The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development publishes a [move-in/move-out inspection form among its landlord forms](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/housing-choice-vouchers-landlord-forms). Its exact program rules are not universal, but the form illustrates a sound records principle: compare condition by area, make space for tenant comments, and distinguish the inspection record from later claims. Your own housing program, state, province or country may require a different form and process.

## Finish with controlled copies and a recoverable backup

The protected record location is an index, not the secret needed to open it. Name the folder, archive or paper envelope where the originals and agreed copies live. Keep the source files at their original resolution, record which version was shared, and avoid overwriting a disputed draft with a later edited copy. If a file is signed elsewhere, preserve the signed original and do not treat a text exported from this generator as a replacement.

After the handover, close every open row in one of three truthful ways: completed with evidence, assigned to a named follow-up, or recorded as unresolved under the applicable process. Then export and test a household backup before deleting location-specific records from a device. The [move-out home records guide](/guides/move-out-home-records/) covers what to transfer, close and keep beyond the physical inspection.

## Privacy and legal boundary

Entries stay in the current browser while the tool runs. Copying, printing, downloading or saving to the local FamilyBoard app creates additional copies under your control, so review them before sharing. Omit full identity numbers, banking details, card numbers, signatures, passwords and access secrets. If an official form requires sensitive information, complete it in the protected channel designated for that process.

This generator cannot decide whether a condition is ordinary wear, whether someone breached a lease, what a repair should cost, whether a notice is valid, or when a deposit must be returned. Those questions depend on the agreement, evidence and law where the home is located. Use current official local guidance or qualified advice for the decision; use this tool for the narrower job of producing a readable, dated and retrievable working record.
