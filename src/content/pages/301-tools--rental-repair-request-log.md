# Rental Repair Request Log

Tenants often need to prove continuity, not make a dramatic claim. A ceiling mark appears, a supplied appliance stops working, a window will not close, or a shared utility fails. The useful record is a calm sequence: what was observed, when the landlord or manager was notified, whether receipt was confirmed, which access window was offered, what work was observed and what remained afterward.

The free Rental Repair Request Log keeps that sequence in one local-first timeline. It is not a lease interpreter, legal notice generator, emergency guide or landlord-contact system. It lets a household point to a protected photo set, lease version, message or service record without copying a full address, lease number, access code, estimate or private conversation into a shared list.

## Start with an observable, bounded problem

Use a neutral code such as `BATHROOM-A` or `FRIDGE-B`. Describe the smallest observable scope and a temporary boundary: “visible water mark; cause not concluded” or “supplied refrigerator cooling issue; do not dismantle.” The request date is when the household actually observed or reported the issue. Do not write a legal conclusion, blame statement, safety certification or assumed repair deadline.

The source field can point to a protected lease equipment list, a dated notice, an authorised communication record or a repair folder. Keep the source pointer short and safe. A landlord's message saying “we received this” is evidence of receipt, not proof that a repair was approved, completed or safe.

## A six-stage request workflow

1. **Observe and protect.** Record what can be seen or safely noticed. Keep photographs and full messages in a protected source; do not approach a hazardous area to improve the record.
2. **Check the communication route.** Point to the current lease or property process and use the agreed channel. The tool does not send a request or verify delivery.
3. **Record notification and receipt separately.** Note when the landlord was notified and when a responsible source confirmed receipt. If there is no confirmation, keep the row open.
4. **Record the access window.** A proposed inspection time, household reply and confirmed appointment are different events. Do not put door codes, exact addresses or personal phone numbers in the shared row.
5. **Observe attendance or work.** Record that a visit or stated action occurred, without certifying workmanship, cause, cost or safety. Use a repair-evidence timeline for a separate before-and-after record.
6. **Review the result.** A later observation may show that the issue remains, changed or needs another source. Create a new version when the scope or responsible role changes rather than rewriting the original request.

## Why a tenant request log is different from a repair history

A general repair history lists work and costs over time. A rental request log preserves the request and communication handoff around a rented home. A building notice log covers an announcement that may affect many residents. These pages can link by safe pointer, but one cannot stand in for another: a contractor invoice does not prove a landlord received a request, and a household reply does not prove a confirmed access appointment.

## Privacy and safety boundaries

Use safe codes instead of a full address, person name, lease number, door code, alarm code, phone number, email, payment detail or private correspondence. The tool does not contact a landlord, calculate a legal deadline, interpret a lease, determine responsibility, approve an expense, grant entry, diagnose a fault, certify a repair or provide legal, rental or safety advice. For fire, gas, electrical, structural, mould or other urgent danger, follow current emergency and qualified-professional instructions before making any record.

## Optional affiliate space remains separate

A future commercial panel could show generic labels or documentation supplies, but it must be clearly disclosed, optional and outside the working form. It cannot select a contractor, promise a landlord response, replace a lease process or make a purchase necessary to create the free request timeline.
