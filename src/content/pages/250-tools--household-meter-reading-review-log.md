# Household Meter Reading Log

Manual readings help a household compare move-in, billing or service-event observations, but a copied meter ID can expose an account and one number cannot explain a provider's billing period. This free browser tool keeps the observation trail small and source-led.

## Record what was visible from a safe position

Choose electricity, water, gas or another service, then record the displayed reading exactly as observed. Do not move heavy covers, open locked boxes, touch wiring or approach gas equipment to complete a row. If the display is unclear, record that condition and leave the row open.

## Date and source matter more than a lone number

Use the date someone actually looked at the display. A safe code may point to a protected photo, provider notice or move-in handoff, but the tool should not contain a meter ID, account number, address or bill image.

## Compare observations without estimating a bill

The output does not subtract readings, apply rates or infer a fault. Differences may reflect a billing period, rollover, changed use, a replacement meter or an estimated reading. Ask the responsible provider about discrepancies.

## Keep move-in, service and outage contexts separate

A handover reading, monthly check, post-repair observation and outage event have different purposes. Give each a safe ID and start a new row when the display or equipment changes.

## Handoff and future affiliate placement

Share the service type, observation date, source code, display condition and next checkpoint—not account-holder names, customer numbers, addresses or payment details. Labels, inspection lights or energy monitors may eventually appear beside this guide, but commercial content cannot guarantee a correct bill or safe installation and must stay outside tool controls.
