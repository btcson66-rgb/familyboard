# How to Use FamilyBoard for a Water Leak Record

“The ceiling is wet” is an important observation, but it is not a diagnosis or a repair result. A household may notice a stain before anyone sees active water, receive a building reply later, move belongings from a dry position and wait for an authorized visit. FamilyBoard can keep those facts in order without guessing where the water came from.

This tutorial pairs with the [Household Water Leak Event Log](/tools/household-water-leak-event-log/). It demonstrates a bounded local workflow: create a private event code, record the earliest supportable observation, index safe evidence, note the responsible source, separate repair work from recovery and assign the next check. It does not inspect plumbing, electricity, structure or moisture, contact a provider, calculate damage, decide insurance or lease responsibility, or provide emergency instructions.

## 1. Stop at the safety boundary

Before opening the form, decide whether the area is safe to observe from where you stand. Do not enter standing water, touch wet outlets or appliances, cross a wet floor to reach a panel, climb under a sagging ceiling or remove a cover to find a source. If there is an immediate hazard, leave and use current local emergency, utility, building or qualified-professional instructions. A row that says `not safely accessible` is a valid record.

The app cannot see the wiring, water depth, building structure or a person's ability to move safely. Never make documentation more important than the observer.

## 2. Create one neutral event code

Use a label such as `LEAK-HOME-A` or `LEAK-2026-01`. Do not put a street address, water account, policy number, phone number, access code or landlord name in the event code. Keep separate events separate: a bathroom drip, a storm-related wall mark and a later appliance hose issue may have different sources, permissions and repair records.

Choose the smallest scope you can support—one area, one fixture group, a shared building location or unresolved scope. Scope describes the observation; it does not assign cause or responsibility.

## 3. Record the first thing someone actually saw

Enter the date and 24-hour time when someone first observed a stain, drip, puddle, odor or other condition. If a photo or message is earlier than the person who noticed it, keep both times and name their sources. Do not reverse-engineer an exact start from the size of a water mark.

Give each affected area an ID such as `CEIL-1`, `FLOOR-1` or `CABINET-1`. Write observable language—“dark mark expanded between two safe observations”—instead of “the upstairs pipe failed.” The tool validates dates and times; it does not infer a source.

## 4. Index evidence without copying sensitive originals

Use a stable pointer such as `LEAK-2026-01-A` for an original photo, building notice, provider message or authorized work order kept elsewhere. The app does not need the original file. Before sharing an export, remove faces, mail, account details, access codes, full addresses and unrelated rooms from the copy.

A photo shows appearance at one moment. It does not prove the path of water, hidden moisture, ownership, negligence or a completed repair. Keep the original unchanged and add later observations rather than replacing the first note.

## 5. Separate notification, finding, work and recovery

Use different rows for the household observation, a notification sent through a verified channel, a qualified or responsible-source finding, authorized work and the later recovery check. “Management contacted” is not the same as “source confirmed.” “Drip stopped” is not the same as “repair completed.” “Surface looks dry” is not a moisture or mold clearance.

Give each open area one owner, one evidence-based action and one review date. A next action can be “check the authorized visit record and add its date,” while “make the room safe” is too broad for a household log. Close only the row whose stated recheck actually occurred.

## 6. Keep the export useful and limited

Review the record before printing or sending it to a family member, manager, landlord, insurer or provider. Share the smallest timeline that answers the recipient's question and keep protected originals under household control. The [water-leak response guide](/guides/water-leak-response-home-records/) explains the broader safety and evidence boundary; the app tutorial focuses on how to operate the local form without turning it into a diagnosis.

## Affiliate boundary

Future recommendations for labels, storage bins or general record supplies may appear after the educational content with a clear disclosure and an easy skip. They cannot appear inside the leak form or result, choose a repair provider, promise drying or change a responsibility status. The workflow remains complete without a purchase.
