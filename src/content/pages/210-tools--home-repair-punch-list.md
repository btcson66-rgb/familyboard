# Home repair punch list

A final walkthrough can fail when every concern is reduced to `finish kitchen` or when a reported correction is treated as verified without another look. A useful punch list preserves one visible item per row, connects it to the controlling scope or change, records what the household actually observed and keeps the item open until the chosen recheck evidence exists.

This free browser tool validates project dates, unique item IDs, scope pointers, observation dates, workflow statuses and target or recheck dates. It counts open items and prints a versioned household review. Processing stays in the browser.

It does not inspect work, diagnose causes, define a defect, issue a completion certificate, accept or reject performance, authorize payment, interpret a contract, verify a permit or inspection, start a warranty, waive rights, determine responsibility or replace a qualified professional.

**Ready for a real walkthrough? [Jump to the Home Repair Punch List](#tool-heading).**

## Freeze the scope version before listing visible items

Enter a private project label rather than a full address. The controlling scope date should point to the signed agreement or accepted estimate that established the work. If approved change orders altered that scope, name their exact IDs in the scope evidence field, for example `CONTRACT-C1 plus approved CHG-1 and CHG-3; excludes dining-room paint`.

The review date is when this version of the household punch list is reconciled. It cannot be in the future or earlier than the scope date. The next review is the latest household checkpoint for open items; it is not a contract, warranty, inspection, payment or legal deadline.

Do not rewrite the scope to match what is visible. The comparison only works when the baseline remains intact and each approved change keeps its own version.

## Use one narrow, observable condition per item

Each row contains ten fields:

`ID | area or element | observable condition | controlling scope or change pointer | observation date | photo or document pointer | next evidence, correction or closure reason | responsible role | target or recheck date | status`

Use stable IDs such as `PL-1`, `PL-2` and `PL-3`. `Kitchen defects` is too broad. `East cabinet door contacts adjacent panel during full opening` is a condition another person can revisit. If the same area has a missing handle and a rubbing door, create two rows because they may have different evidence, owners and close dates.

Write the work area and responsible roles without private contact information. `Kitchen east wall`, `household project owner` and `contractor project lead` are sufficient for a shared record.

## Separate observation from diagnosis, standard and responsibility

A household record may say `three floor tiles show visible edge-height differences under the fixed hallway light; PHOTO-22`. It should not turn that observation into `unsafe installation`, `code violation`, `manufacturer defect` or `contractor negligence` without naming the qualified or responsible source that made that determination.

The controlling pointer may be a drawing, product schedule, change order or written scope. If no source defines an expected condition, record that evidence gap rather than inventing a standard. A punch-list row organizes the question; it does not supply the answer.

If there is fire, gas odor, exposed energized equipment, structural movement, contaminated water, injury or another immediate hazard, leave the area and follow local emergency, utility, building and qualified-professional instructions. Do not approach danger for a photo or keep using this tool before urgent action.

## Observation dates and evidence pointers must stay attributable

The observation date must fall between the controlling scope date and this review date. Use a protected pointer such as `PHOTO-18`, `VIDEO-3`, `WALKTHROUGH-NOTE-2` or `INSPECTION-R4`; do not paste the original file, person's private contact details or a full address into the shared row.

A photo supports visible appearance at a particular date and angle. It usually cannot prove concealed work, code compliance, structural or electrical safety, material identity, moisture behind a finish or the cause of a condition. An inspection record supports only what its issuer actually wrote. Preserve issuer, date, scope and full original separately.

## Five statuses keep reports and rechecks distinct

`Observed—written response pending` means the visible item has been recorded but the next written response or evidence is still open.

`Correction planned—not rechecked` means a plan or commitment is referenced, but the household has not observed the reported result at its chosen evidence level.

`Provider reports corrected—recheck pending` preserves the provider's report without converting it into household verification.

`Closed—dated recheck evidence linked` means the household linked its chosen dated recheck evidence and closed the organizational row. It does not certify workmanship, hidden conditions, inspection, safety, contract acceptance or legal effect.

`Archived—not pursued, reason recorded` preserves why an item is no longer in the active queue without pretending it was corrected. The reason may be a documented scope exclusion, a superseding approved change or a household decision made after obtaining the appropriate information.

## Open and closed rows use dates differently

The first three statuses remain open. Their target or recheck date must fall on or after the punch-list review date and no later than the next household review. Use a next step that identifies the evidence needed to change status, such as `obtain written response against DRAWING-A3` or `recheck full door travel and preserve dated photo after reported adjustment`.

Closed and archived rows use the last field as the actual recheck or archive date. That date must fall between the original observation and this review. The evidence or closure-reason field must explain why the row is no longer open; `done` and `fixed` are not enough.

These dates organize household follow-up. They do not extend a contract notice, cure period, payment, lien, permit, inspection, insurance, complaint, warranty or other deadline. Retrieve those dates from the controlling current source.

## Keep completion, acceptance, payment and warranty outside the status field

The [Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-home-improvement-scam) recommends detailed written contracts, preserving written promises and tying payment to completed work. The [New York City home-improvement consumer guide](https://a860-gpp.nyc.gov/downloads/cr56n228m?locale=en) recommends inspecting the work carefully and using the checklist that formed the basis of estimates before final payment. California publishes a separate [homeowner checklist](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/Homeowner_Checklists.aspx) with state-specific contract and payment rules.

Those sources show why a dated list matters, but they do not create one universal final-payment rule. Contract completion, household satisfaction, authority inspection, legal acceptance, lien protection, warranty commencement and payment authorization can be distinct events. A FamilyBoard status cannot decide or merge them.

## Reconcile the punch list with the full project archive

At each review, compare item IDs against the unchanged baseline, all approved or declined changes, provider responses, dated evidence and current invoice. If an item is actually a new request outside the controlling scope, move it into the [Home Repair Change Order Log](/tools/home-repair-change-order-log/) rather than disguising it as a correction.

At project close-out, retain the final punch-list version alongside contracts, change orders, payments, permits, inspection reports, product schedules, manuals, warranties, before-and-after observations and unresolved items. Read the [renovation records guide](/guides/renovation-records/) for the full chronology.

## Protect private household and dispute information

The form blocks common full phone, email, address, credential, financial, government-ID, licence, policy, claim, signature and private-contact patterns. Automated screening cannot identify every sensitive fact, so inspect the result before copying, printing or saving it.

Use roles and protected pointers in the shared output. Keep originals, signatures, complete provider details, transaction records, access information and dispute files in appropriately controlled storage. If a disagreement develops, preserve previous versions rather than rewriting the list into an accusation or a final legal position.

## Commercial recommendations stay outside walkthrough decisions

A future affiliate area may show clearly labelled measuring tools, lights, document storage or project software beside this public page. It cannot appear inside the form or result, close an item, change its evidence, authorize payment or imply that a sponsored provider or product satisfies the project scope.
