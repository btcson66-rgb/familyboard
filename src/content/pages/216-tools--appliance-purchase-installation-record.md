# Appliance purchase and installation record

A major appliance can produce five different sources before normal use begins: the order or sales contract, receipt or final invoice, delivery record, installer report and written warranty. If those documents are reduced to one line such as `washer bought August 2026`, the household loses the evidence needed to answer which unit arrived, whether installation was included, which date the warranty says controls and what remained unresolved after first use.

This free browser tool creates a source-linked activation record. It does not verify a seller or installer, inspect delivery condition, determine correct installation, interpret a warranty, calculate a legal deadline, register a product, submit a claim or decide whether an appliance is safe to use.

**Adding a household appliance? [Jump to the Appliance Purchase and Installation Record](#tool-heading).**

## Use a private asset ID before copying purchase data

Choose a stable household ID such as `LAUNDRY-WASHER-A3`. Preserve the public brand and model reference separately, and keep the complete serial number or label photograph behind a protected pointer such as `SERIAL-PHOTO-S1`. A retailer order number, invoice number, financing account or delivery address should not become the asset ID.

This separation lets a printable or shared household handoff identify the appliance without exposing identifiers that may be reused in service, warranty, account or recall processes.

## Keep acquisition, possession and activation dates separate

The tool asks for three chronology anchors:

1. **Purchase, contract or household-acquisition date:** when the known transaction or transfer began.
2. **Delivery or possession date:** when this household received the physical unit.
3. **Installation or first-use date:** when it was installed or first put into ordinary household use.

They may be the same for a small appliance. They may differ for contractor-supplied equipment, delayed delivery or a replacement unit. The tool checks their order but does not decide which one starts a warranty. Copy the start method from the written terms and preserve uncertainty if the source is missing or unclear.

## Use a ten-field evidence row

Each versioned row has this format:

`ID | evidence stage | attributable product, purchase, delivery, installation, warranty or recheck fact | actor or source role | event date | protected evidence pointer | next gap or closure reason | owner role | target or outcome date | status`

Use safe IDs such as `BUY-1`, `DELIVERY-D1` and `INSTALL-I1`. Do not paste complete serial, order, invoice, account, card, address, contact, signature or access details into the shareable row.

## Nine statuses prevent a receipt from pretending to be activation

The accepted states are:

1. **Purchase source recorded—delivery pending:** the known order, contract or transfer source exists; physical receipt is not implied.
2. **Delivery received—condition and contents review pending:** possession is sourced; household review remains open.
3. **Installation arranged—installer outcome pending:** the appointment or scope is documented; work is not implied complete.
4. **Installation source recorded—household recheck pending:** an attributable installer completion source exists; household first use remains open.
5. **Warranty start basis pending—written terms needed:** dates exist, but the controlling start method is missing or unresolved.
6. **Active record—identity, receipt, warranty basis and household recheck linked:** the ordinary activation evidence set is connected.
7. **Limited archive—missing source named and ownership assigned:** the household has preserved an honest incomplete record without inventing facts.
8. **Transferred or gifted—origin and coverage uncertainty preserved:** possession changed and unknown original terms remain explicit.
9. **Returned, cancelled or replaced—outcome source linked:** the original transaction no longer activates this household asset.

The first five remain open and need a target date from the current review through the next evidence checkpoint. The final four require an actual outcome date from acquisition through the current review. These dates manage household follow-up only; they are not return, warranty, statutory, tax, complaint or limitation deadlines.

## The written warranty owns its start method

The [FTC's current warranty guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties) recommends saving both the warranty and product receipt and distinguishes a separately purchased service contract from the warranty included with a product. Record the exact source and stated start method rather than typing a guessed duration or letting the tool choose purchase, delivery or installation automatically.

Keep product warranty, paid service contract, retailer protection plan, credit-card benefit and installer workmanship promise as separate source pointers. A single end date without provider, scope and start basis hides which promise is supposed to apply.

## Delivery evidence is narrower than household acceptance

A carrier scan or signed delivery source can show that something was delivered. It may not prove the exact model, included parts, concealed condition, installation or satisfactory operation unless the source actually states those facts. Add a separate dated household observation for packaging, visible product identity, components and first ordinary use.

Do not reproduce a hazardous condition to complete a row. If there is smoke, fire, fuel or gas odor, electric shock, overheating, serious leak, damaged power connection, injury or a product-safety notice, stop the routine record and follow current responsible-authority, manufacturer, emergency and qualified-professional instructions.

## Installer evidence does not certify workmanship

For installed equipment, preserve who supplied the unit, who installed it, the attributable scope, any permit or commissioning pointer that actually exists and the household recheck. FamilyBoard cannot verify licensing, insurance, identity, code compliance, settings, connection quality, leak testing, electrical work, gas work or safety.

If the installed model differs from the proposal or order, preserve both sources and open an evidence gap. Do not silently edit the proposal to match what arrived or declare substitution authorized without an attributable approval source.

## Gifts, transfers and appliances already in the home need an honest baseline

For a gift, inherited unit, used purchase, rental appliance or appliance included with a home, use the known household-acquisition date and identify the source role. Leave original purchase, ownership and warranty-transfer details unknown unless a controlling document supports them.

The `Limited archive` and `Transferred or gifted` states exist so the tool can produce a useful record without pressuring the household to invent a receipt, first-use date, original owner or warranty entitlement.

## Keep electronic receipts outside the shareable output

Save a durable copy or protected pointer to the receipt, invoice or order source. Do not paste complete transaction numbers, account details, loyalty identifiers or payment data into the tool. Retailer portals and email accounts can change; the household index should still show what source exists and where the protected original lives.

The receipt is useful evidence but not the entire appliance history. Link the written warranty, manual, delivery, installation and household recheck separately.

## Model and serial evidence support later service and recall work

CPSC recall notices often define affected products by model and serial ranges. Preserve a protected label photo early, before installation makes the plate difficult to reach. Do not move, open or dismantle connected equipment just to obtain it, and do not let this tool claim that a product matches or clears a recall.

If an authoritative notice appears later, use a separate [Product Recall Action Log](/tools/product-recall-action-log/) to record the comparison and remedy source. The purchase record remains the historical origin layer.

## Privacy screening is a backstop

The tool blocks common full phone and email patterns, long numeric identifiers, addresses, access instructions, full serial and transaction numbers, financial accounts, credentials, signatures, private contacts and complaint or legal material. Automated screening cannot identify every sensitive fact.

Review every field before copying, printing, downloading or sharing. FamilyBoard processes this working record in the current browser and does not receive or back up the original purchase documents.

## Affiliate placement cannot change activation status

A future labelled affiliate area may show document sleeves, label makers, receipt scanners or general household record supplies. It cannot choose an appliance, seller or installer; verify delivery or installation; interpret coverage; calculate a return or warranty deadline; determine recall status; or mark the record active merely because a product was bought through a link.
