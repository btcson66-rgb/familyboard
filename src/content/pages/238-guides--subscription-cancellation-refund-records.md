# Subscription Cancellation and Refund Records

An unwanted renewal is rarely one event. A household may have an offer, a trial, terms, a renewal notice, a cancellation request, a confirmation, a refund request and a later card or bank statement. Treating all of those as “the subscription” makes it hard to tell whether a service was cancelled, whether a refund was promised, and whether a charge actually stopped. This guide gives you a source-first record structure without collecting payment credentials.

## Separate the six records

Keep six references: the offer or trial, the current terms and price, the renewal or charge notice, the cancellation action, the merchant response, and the actual account result. Use safe codes such as `OFFER-A1`, `TERMS-T1`, `CANCEL-C1` and `RESULT-R1`. Store the original screens, emails and statements in a protected location; FamilyBoard only needs the code, date and status.

The Federal Trade Commission advises consumers to keep a copy of a cancellation request and notes about conversations when a recurring service does not stop. That is a record-keeping practice, not a guarantee that every service or jurisdiction follows the same cancellation rule. The terms presented at sign-up, later changes, the merchant’s channel and local law may all matter.

## A cancellation request is not the same as confirmation

Write down how the request was made—account setting, web form, email, phone or another channel—and the confirmation reference if one exists. Do not paste the full message or a phone transcript into a general household tool. Record whether the merchant acknowledged the request, whether the account showed a future renewal state, and what remains unconfirmed.

If the service requires a particular channel, use the current instructions shown in the account or agreement. Do not infer that deleting an app, removing a payment method or stopping use cancelled the contract. Those actions may be relevant evidence, but only the responsible service can confirm the account state.

## Refund requests need their own evidence chain

A refund request, an approval, a refund sent and a refund posted are different states. Give each a separate code and keep the merchant’s original instructions in protected storage. Never enter a card number, bank account, full receipt or transaction identifier in the browser worksheet. If a merchant asks for sensitive information, use its authenticated channel and record only that the handoff occurred.

Do not calculate a chargeback deadline from a generic article. A card network, bank, merchant agreement and local consumer process can have different instructions. Read the current notice and ask the responsible financial or consumer service when timing matters.

## Organize a repeated-charge follow-up

Review the next statement or account event from an appropriate source, then mark the actual result. If a second charge appears, link it to the cancellation code but do not merge the two events. Note the merchant’s response, the payment provider’s route and any unresolved question as separate rows. This prevents a household summary from accidentally claiming that a charge was unauthorized, a refund was owed or a dispute was won.

## What the free FamilyBoard workflow does not do

It does not sign in to a merchant, cancel a service, contact a bank, authenticate a receipt, calculate a legal or card-network deadline, decide whether a charge is valid, or file a dispute. It is a private index for source versions, requests and outcomes. Use the merchant, payment provider, card issuer, bank, consumer authority or qualified adviser that applies to the actual transaction.

## Keep commercial recommendations separate

Future affiliate links for general budgeting folders, receipt scanners or offline storage may be useful in a neutral “organize your records” area. They must not imply that a product can cancel a subscription, stop a charge, obtain a refund or win a dispute. Keep commercial content away from urgent payment or complaint instructions.

### Frequently asked questions

#### Does deleting the app cancel the subscription?

Not necessarily. Verify the service’s current cancellation route and obtain a confirmation from the responsible account or merchant source.

#### Should I remove my card from the account first?

Do not assume that will cancel an agreement. Follow the current terms and keep payment details out of the FamilyBoard worksheet.

#### Is an email saying “refund approved” proof that I received the money?

No. Preserve it as an approval record and wait for an appropriate statement or account source to show the actual result.

#### Can I use this guide to file a chargeback?

No. It helps organize evidence only. Use the current instructions from the card issuer, bank, payment provider or consumer authority.

## Source notes

The record structure follows current FTC consumer advice on free trials, auto-renewals and keeping cancellation requests. It is intentionally jurisdiction-neutral and does not turn that advice into a universal refund or deadline rule.
