# Pantry and Household Consumables Expiry Review Log

Pantry checks become easier when a household can see which source was checked, where an item belongs and who will look again. This free browser tool creates a small, versioned review record without requiring names, addresses, receipts, payment data or private messages.

## Record an observation, not a safety verdict

Use an item or category code, the label or source date, a storage-zone code, a package or quantity observation and a planned next step. The result is a household index. It does not decide whether anything is safe, edible, expired, suitable for a person or ready to discard.

## Separate the label from the plan

A printed date, a manufacturer instruction and a family meal plan are different sources. Keep them as separate references. If a label is unclear, a package is damaged or a household member has a question, leave the row open and identify the responsible source instead of guessing.

## Use rotation as a follow-up task

Rotation can mean adding an item to a planned meal, moving it to a clearly labelled zone or asking the household shopping role to check it. Record the owner and next date. Do not use a generic online list to invent a storage period for a particular product.

## Keep private purchase details out

Do not paste delivery addresses, loyalty numbers, payment details, full receipts, private family messages or medical and allergy histories. A safe code can point to protected records, while the public-facing or shared output stays useful without exposing information that other household members do not need.

## Review by a small zone

Start with one shelf, drawer or refrigerator zone and no more than twelve rows. A small scope makes it possible to physically check the package, update the observation and close the follow-up. If the household has a larger inventory, create another dated review rather than producing an unmaintained master list.

## Future affiliate placement

Labels, storage bins or notebooks may eventually be recommended beside this guide, but a product cannot interpret a date or guarantee food safety. Any commercial block must remain outside the tool controls and must never replace the label, manufacturer, local authority or qualified source.
