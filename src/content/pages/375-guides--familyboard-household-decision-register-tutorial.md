# How to use FamilyBoard's household decision register without losing the reasoning

Household choices disappear easily into a chat thread: someone asked whether to repair or replace an appliance, another person shared a quote, and weeks later nobody remembers which constraint mattered. FamilyBoard's [Household Decision Register](/tools/household-decision-register/) keeps one bounded question, the source to reopen, the options or constraints considered, the accountable role and the observed result together. It does not vote, rank products, access accounts or decide legal, medical, financial or safety questions.

## Frame one question and one decision boundary

Start with a question that can be answered or deliberately left open: “Should we book the authorised repair after the written estimate is reviewed?” is clearer than “What should we do about the washer?” Define what is inside the row and what belongs elsewhere. A repair choice may link to a protected estimate and service history; a payment or warranty claim needs its own controlling record.

Use a neutral reference such as `DECISION-2026-A`. Do not use a person’s full name, address, account number, policy number or private message as the ID. A decision row is a coordination label, not a permission or identity system.

## Name the source and the constraints separately

Record the source that will be reopened: a current manual, written quote, lease clause, provider notice, household budget record or meeting note. Then list the real constraints without turning them into a conclusion: available time, access, noise, accessibility, budget ceiling, rental approval or the need to keep an old part for evidence. A constraint is not a guarantee that an option is affordable, safe or legally allowed.

Keep the original file, contract, invoice, photo and private conversation in protected storage. The register needs a pointer such as `QUOTE-Q2 / protected repair folder` and a short observation, not a copied document or credential. If two sources conflict, preserve both pointers and assign a reviewer instead of choosing the most convenient wording.

## Separate options from the decision and the result

Use the options field to describe what the household actually considered: repair now, obtain another authorised quote, defer while monitoring or replace after a documented review. Do not present a sponsored product or a family guess as a neutral option. The deciding role records the choice and its date; a later row or linked evidence records what happened.

“Repair selected” is a decision. “Appointment requested” is an action. “Technician report received” is a source result. “Appliance operated normally at the dated recheck” is an observation. Keeping those stages separate prevents a decision from masquerading as completed work, payment, cancellation or safety certification.

## Give unresolved decisions one next checkpoint

An open decision should name one next action, one accountable role and a realistic date. “Review the current quote and confirm whether the warranty source applies” is testable. “Think about it” is not. If the decision depends on another person’s consent, an official reply or a qualified inspection, keep that dependency visible and do not mark the row final before it arrives.

The date is a household planning checkpoint, not a legal deadline or service guarantee. Retrieve notice, contract, benefit, tax, warranty and complaint dates from their controlling sources. FamilyBoard can remind a person when they reopen the app; it does not send background alerts.

## Reopen the register when the facts change

Create a dated follow-up when a quote changes, a provider issues a new notice, a member's availability changes, a repair reveals a new condition or the household's constraint is different. Preserve the earlier row so a later reader can see what was known at that time. Do not edit history until an old decision looks tidy.

At a monthly or household meeting review, ask whether the source is still current, whether the deciding role still has authority, what result was actually observed and what remains open. Link the [household meeting tutorial](/guides/familyboard-household-meeting-tutorial/) when several questions need a shared agenda, but keep each decision bounded so one unresolved issue does not hide inside a long transcript.

## Privacy, handoff and affiliate boundaries

Before sharing a register, remove exact addresses, account details, payment information, medical narratives, private correspondence and unnecessary names. A downloaded result becomes a new copy even though the tool processes typed values locally. Give the next role the safe ID, source pointer, current constraint and next checkpoint; do not hand over passwords or full documents in chat.

Future affiliate suggestions for tools, appliances, storage or services must remain outside the decision form, clearly labelled and optional. A commission cannot choose an option, prove a quote, close a decision or make a household purchase necessary to use the free register.
