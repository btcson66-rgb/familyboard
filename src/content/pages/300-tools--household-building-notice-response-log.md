# Household Building Notice Response Log

Notices arrive through many channels: a building manager posts a repair announcement, a landlord proposes an inspection window, a utility provider warns of an interruption, or a shared facility changes its access hours. The message may be short, but the household still needs to know when it arrived, which area it affects, what response was sent and whether the responsible source confirmed the next step.

The free Building Notice Response Log keeps those facts in order. It is a source and handoff index, not a legal interpretation, permission system or emergency guide. You can point to a protected notice, lease version, building announcement or public authority page without copying its private contents into a shared household list.

## Separate the notice, the request and the result

Begin with a neutral notice or location code such as `BUILDING-A` or `RENTAL-B`. In the impact field, describe the smallest observable scope: “basement water shutoff announcement; kitchen supply may be affected” or “inspection window offered; exact access process remains with the agreement.” Avoid declaring that a notice is valid, that a fee is owed, that entry is authorised or that a repair is safe.

The received date is when the household actually saw or received the notice. A source pointer can identify a protected PDF, posted announcement, dated lease version, service portal message or public authority page. The observation field should say whether the source requested an acknowledgement, a time window, a preparation, a payment, a temporary move or another response. A household reply is not the same as source confirmation, and a scheduled visit is not proof that work occurred.

## A five-pass notice workflow

1. **Capture the smallest useful fact.** Record the received date, affected area and source pointer. Do not copy the entire message or include an address, access code, phone number or private participant detail.
2. **Map the controlling source.** Link the relevant agreement, building rule, provider source or public authority using a protected pointer. If sources disagree, keep both pointers and leave the conflict open.
3. **Describe the requested response.** State whether the household acknowledged, asked a clarification, proposed a time, declined, prepared an area or is waiting for a responsible source. Do not convert a request into consent.
4. **Record the household action.** Date the action that actually happened, such as a reply being sent or a safe preparation being completed. Keep emergency and technical instructions with the responsible professional source.
5. **Wait for confirmation and review.** A source confirmation, attendance, work result or revised notice is a separate event. Create a new version when the scope, date, responsible role or source changes so the earlier notice remains auditable.

## Useful for renters, owners and shared homes

Renters can use the log to distinguish a landlord's proposed inspection from a confirmed appointment and to point back to the protected agreement. Owners can keep property-manager, building committee and service-provider announcements separate from maintenance work records. Shared homes can assign a household role to check the source without putting personal phone numbers or door information into the common row.

The log also connects cleanly to other FamilyBoard pages. A repair-evidence timeline can describe the observable condition before and after work. An insurance timeline can capture a separate incident and insurer response. A contractor record can hold service details. The notice log answers a narrower question: what was announced, what did the household reply, and which source still needs to confirm?

## Privacy, access and safety boundaries

Use safe codes instead of full addresses, names, telephone numbers, email addresses, lease numbers, door codes, alarm codes, payment details or private correspondence. The tool does not send messages, grant entry, verify consent, calculate a legal deadline, interpret a lease, decide a property manager's authority, certify a repair or provide legal, rental or safety advice. Do not approach a hazardous work area or change locks, wiring, plumbing or building systems to complete a record. Follow current emergency, property, provider and qualified-professional instructions for real decisions.

It cannot rank a landlord, choose a contractor, promise legal protection or make a purchase necessary to create and review this free log.
