# Repetition is a good reason to build a system—but not every repeated action needs a notification

The best recurring tasks are responsibilities people routinely forget, hand off or need to coordinate. A recurrence should remove a real memory or scheduling failure; it should not turn every ordinary habit into a notification. Start by observing what was late, duplicated or argued about during the last month, then add only those items to the system.

For each recurring item, record the task, owner, trigger, expected result and the source that proves the result. “Check the water filter” is an instruction. “Replace when the indicator changes; record the cartridge size and installation date” is a repeatable rule. A clear definition of done prevents a household from counting a reminder as completion.

## Daily and weekly

Cleaning, laundry, waste, shopping and pet routines may need shared visibility, especially when responsibility rotates. Use a fixed day when an outside schedule controls the work—trash pickup, school collection or a building inspection. Use a completion-based interval when the work depends on use or the last service—watering a plant every ten days or cleaning a filter after a measured amount of use.

Avoid creating a task for something everyone already does without coordination. If a daily habit never needs a reminder, keep it out of the shared queue. The value of a recurring list is signal: the people opening it should be able to see what needs a decision or a handoff, not scroll past dozens of automatic facts.

## Monthly and quarterly

Household reviews, filter inspections, supply checks, subscription reviews and document updates are more likely to benefit from reminders. Put the responsible role and a review window in the task, not just a month name. A monthly subscription scan may be due before a renewal notice, while a quarterly safety check may need to happen after a seasonal change. Keep the reason visible so the next owner can adjust the date without guessing.

Some review tasks produce a new record rather than a physical result. Record the source checked, the date observed and the decision made. “Review insurance” is not complete evidence; “checked the current policy source, no change found, next review 2026-11-01” is a traceable outcome.

## Seasonal and annual

Maintenance, renewals, registrations, planned service and emergency-plan reviews belong here. Set a preparation date before the actual deadline when the provider, building or government process needs lead time. A seasonal task should name the condition that starts it—before the first cold week, after the rainy season, or when the building posts its inspection notice—rather than pretending every climate and property follows the same calendar.

Annual tasks need extra protection against drift. Keep the last confirmed source, the responsible role and the next notice date. If a household move, new appliance, new pet or new caregiver changes the obligation, retire the old recurrence and create the new one; do not quietly edit history until it looks as if the new rule always existed.

## Use the right trigger

Some tasks should recur after completion rather than on a fixed calendar date. Others depend on season, mileage, usage or an external renewal date. When the trigger is uncertain, write the observation that will decide it: an indicator light, a meter reading, a provider notice or a last-completed event. FamilyBoard stores the recurrence description as household context; it does not automatically measure every device, contact every provider or infer that a task was done.

Review the list after two cycles. If a reminder fires before anyone can act, move the trigger earlier or split preparation from execution. If a task keeps being postponed because nobody owns the decision, it is a responsibility-design problem rather than a calendar problem. Assign an owner and an escalation role before adding more reminders.

## Keep recurring work private and portable

Do not place passwords, full account numbers, access codes or private medical notes inside a repeat description. Use a neutral source ID and store the protected original where the authorised person can reach it. FamilyBoard records stay in the current browser, so export and test a backup before clearing site data or changing devices. A printed recurring list should include its prepared date and be retired when a household change invalidates it.

Future recommendations for labels, planners or storage products must remain outside the task instructions, clearly disclosed and optional. A household should be able to run its recurring system with the free App alone.
