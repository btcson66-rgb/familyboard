# Home care service plan change and notice log

A home-care change can arrive as a new order, reassessment, agency scheduling message, program authorization, contract amendment, coverage notice, worker transition or service-ending notice. Those events are not interchangeable. A phone call may announce a proposal but not amend the responsible plan. A signed receipt may show delivery but not agreement. A stated effective date may pass before the first changed visit actually occurs.

This free browser tool creates a dated household index for the change signal, responsible program or agency source, protected person match, before-and-after plan or contract versions, change authority and reason category, effective service batch, formal notice and delivery, available copy, accessibility need, person participation, household response or disagreement, transition arrangement, first changed-service result, responsible owner and reopen condition.

The log is not a care plan, order, contract amendment, legal notice, acknowledgment, consent, appeal, complaint or proof that a change is valid. It does not contact an agency; change or terminate care; sign, deliver or contest a notice; determine coverage; calculate a response or appeal deadline; guarantee replacement service; or decide whether a person agreed. The current ordering professional, program, payer, agency, contract, official notice and review process control the real change.

**Need to replace `the agency said the schedule is changing` with a source-controlled change record? [Jump to the Home Care Service Plan Change and Notice Log](#tool-heading).**

## Start with the change relationship, not the message

Use a private code such as `HOME-CARE-CHANGE-2026-A`. Choose the actual household context: reassessment or plan change; contract or private-pay scope change; worker or service-window transition; reduction, termination or increase; agency or program transition; cost or coverage notice; or disagreement and continuity follow-up.

The baseline date identifies the last source version used before the proposed change. The current review date shows when the household actually checked the source pointers. The next checkpoint limits unresolved household follow-up. None of these dates replaces a notice-issued date, effective date, service date or filing deadline.

Map the responsible sources with safe identifiers. The map may include an ordering or case-management source, public program, agency, old and current plan, contract or attachment, formal notice and official review route. Keep names, beneficiary or case numbers, medical content, exact schedules, signatures and notice text in the protected systems that own them.

## Each row preserves twelve different claims

Enter one proposed or implemented change per line:

`ID | safe care-person alias and change context | responsible program, agency, ordering, case-management or notice source | protected person-match evidence plus source checked date YYYY-MM-DD | before-and-after plan, contract, authorization or notice versions | change authority, reason category and effective service batch | formal notice type, delivery, available copy and accessibility need | person participation, household response, receipt or disagreement result | transition, service continuity, first changed service or official review result | owner role | target or outcome date YYYY-MM-DD | one of the twelve exact statuses`

The protected source-check date must fall between the baseline and current review. The first nine statuses remain open and need a target between the current review and next checkpoint. The last three close only this household version and need an observed result date no later than the current review. One version accepts no more than 14 unique rows.

A safe row can say `before version P2 and approved current version P3 opened`, `formal notice N3 delivered; copy available`, `household confirmed receipt and asked a transition question; not agreement`, or `first changed-service result observed in responsible agency source`. Do not paste an order, care plan, notice reason narrative, signature, exact service window, health fact, billing value or appeal argument.

## Twelve statuses stop one “changed” checkbox from hiding gaps

1. **Home-care service change signal received—change context pending:** a message or event exists, but the household has not identified what kind of change it is.
2. **Change context recorded—responsible program, agency or notice source pending:** the change category is known while the source that can issue or confirm it remains unresolved.
3. **Responsible change source recorded—protected person match pending:** a program, agency or ordering source is known, but the source has not been matched to the intended person through a protected process.
4. **Protected person match recorded—before-and-after plan, contract, authorization or notice versions pending:** the safe match exists while the actual controlling versions remain open.
5. **Before-and-after versions recorded—change authority, reason category and effective service batch pending:** documents were found, but the accountable issuer and service batch are not yet attributable.
6. **Change authority, reason and effective batch recorded—formal notice, delivery, copy and accessibility pending:** the proposed implementation is known while notice handling remains incomplete.
7. **Formal notice and delivery recorded—person participation, household response or disagreement result pending:** delivery is documented, but the response must not be inferred from receipt.
8. **Participation, response or disagreement recorded—transition and first changed-service result pending:** a response exists while replacement, continuity or first implementation remains unresolved.
9. **Version, notice, effective date, service continuity, cost or rights conflict—program, complaint or qualified review pending:** sources disagree and a named responsible route must decide.
10. **Change source, versions, notice, response, transition and first changed-service result reviewed:** every layer is linked, the official review route and reopen rule are known and no conflict remains hidden.
11. **Responsible change result received—custody, service continuity and reopen condition recorded:** an attributable result was observed and its protected custody and future trigger are documented.
12. **Not applicable—reason and reopen event recorded:** the change does not apply now, and the plan, contract, notice, service or program event that would reopen it is named.

The status describes the household's evidence position, not the legal or clinical validity of the change. A row can be fully reviewed while the household still disagrees with the outcome. Preserve the responsible decision and the next official route instead of changing the status to make the result look favorable.

## A change signal, approved version, notice and first changed visit are separate

Use this sequence:

1. **Signal:** someone proposes, predicts or reports a change.
2. **Responsible-source decision:** the ordering professional, case manager, program, agency or contract process issues an attributable result.
3. **Version:** the old and new plan, authorization, contract or attachment can be distinguished.
4. **Notice:** the required or contractual notice is issued through the responsible channel.
5. **Participation and response:** the person and household have an opportunity to receive, understand, ask, agree, decline or disagree as the applicable process allows.
6. **Transition:** replacement, schedule, handoff and continuity steps are identified.
7. **Implementation:** the first service batch under the new version actually occurs or fails to occur.
8. **Review result:** any disagreement, complaint or appeal receives an attributable outcome.

Do not jump from step one to step seven. A scheduler's call cannot prove that an authorization changed. A notice cannot prove that the first changed service happened. A household acceptance does not prove that the agency implemented the change, and a completed visit does not prove that the correct notice or rights process occurred.

Use the [Home Care Visit Scope and Service Result Log](/tools/home-care-visit-scope-service-result-log/) for the separate visit evidence, service-scope exception and responsible agency result. Use this page for the change chain that controls which version should apply.

## HHCCN, ABN and NOMNC are not three names for one letter

The [CMS Home Health Change of Care Notice page](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/forms-notices/beneficiary-notices-initiative/ffs-hhccn) says Form CMS-10280 is used to notify Original Medicare beneficiaries receiving home-health benefits of plan-of-care changes and that written notification is required before an agency reduces or terminates an item or service when the notice is required. This is a specific Original Medicare process, not a universal form for every home-care arrangement.

The current [Medicare protections page](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/your-medicare-rights/your-protections) distinguishes an Advance Beneficiary Notice for certain possible noncoverage situations, an HHCCN for certain home-health changes, and a Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage when covered services are ending. Each notice has its own purpose and instructions. The household should record the exact notice type from the responsible source rather than relabeling every agency email `HHCCN`.

A state Medicaid program, Medicaid managed-care plan, Medicare Advantage plan, private insurer, private-duty contract or community program can use different notices and review routes. The tool does not decide which form applies. If the source is unclear, keep the row open at status two or nine and obtain the answer from the responsible agency, plan, program, contract or qualified assistance source.

## Receipt, acknowledgment, agreement and waiver are different

`Delivered`, `opened`, `copy received`, `signature captured`, `questions asked`, `agreed`, `declined` and `appeal filed` are separate events. A form can require acknowledgment of receipt without turning that acknowledgment into agreement with the change. The tool therefore records delivery and household response in separate columns.

Use factual language: `copy received through responsible portal`, `accessibility request sent`, `household asked which service batch is first`, or `disagreement route identified`. Never type a signature, mark a person as consenting because the agency retained a copy, or claim that silence waived a right.

If the person needs another format, language support, a paper copy or help understanding the notice, record only a safe request/result pointer. The agency, program or qualified assistance source—not FamilyBoard—must provide the real accessible material and determine what the process requires.

## An effective date does not prove implementation

An authorization can say a version is effective on one date while the agency schedules the first changed visit later. A contract attachment can be signed while an old schedule remains temporarily in use. A termination notice can state an end date while a separate continuity or appeal process is underway.

Record an **effective service batch**, such as `BATCH-C` or `WINDOW-D`, rather than copying the person's exact calendar. Then connect that batch to the first responsible service result. If the first changed visit is missed, shortened or still uses the old scope, keep the change row open and create a related visit exception in the visit log.

Do not backdate a household row to make the paperwork and service appear synchronized. Preserve the date each source was observed and let the responsible program or agency correct its own records.

## Worker changes and plan changes may overlap without being the same

A different worker can deliver the same authorized service under the same plan. A different service window can require only an agency schedule change. A reassessment can change the plan, contract attachment, authorization and schedule together. The household must identify which layer actually changed.

For a worker or schedule transition, use the [Caregiver Handoff Source and Authorization Log](/tools/caregiver-handoff-source-authorization-log/) to verify recipient authority, minimum information scope and actual access. Do not copy a worker name, employee identifier, background check, private contact or exact route into the change log.

Replacement and uninterrupted service are outcomes, not promises. Record who is responsible, the proposed transition, the first changed-service result and any official review route. FamilyBoard cannot arrange a replacement, promise coverage or certify staffing.

## Keep cost, coverage, service and rights questions in separate lanes

A plan change may alter service frequency. A payer notice may address coverage. A contract amendment may change private-pay terms. An agency invoice may reflect actual delivery. A complaint may concern quality or continuity. One source cannot silently decide all five.

Record safe pointers to each responsible route. Do not calculate what Medicare, Medicaid, an insurer, public program or private contract should pay. Do not convert a quoted amount into an eligibility or coverage conclusion. Preserve the official notice and bill outside the tool, then use the process named in the current document.

If a service reduction, ending or cost issue is time-sensitive, read the actual notice immediately. The tool intentionally does not store or calculate the deadline because notice types, programs, jurisdictions and facts differ. A household reminder is not an extension of an official timeframe.

## Privacy is part of a defensible change record

The workbench rejects patterns that resemble names, contacts, full addresses, long case or member numbers, exact visit times or locations, diagnoses, symptoms, medicines, care tasks, notice or appeal text, costs, signatures, credentials and private correspondence. It requires twelve complete fields, unique row IDs, valid date windows and evidence appropriate to reviewed, transition-pending, conflict, completed and not-applicable statuses.

Validation is a drafting guard, not proof. A safe code can still refer to the wrong person or document. Compare every result with the current protected source, correct the source owner rather than the household copy and store the output where only the intended household roles can access it.

## Affiliate products stay outside service and rights decisions

A future affiliate area may show clearly labeled general document folders, labels, scanners or offline storage after the full workflow. It must not interrupt notice reading, a time-sensitive rights route, a service-continuity problem or an urgent safety action. It must not target health or care details, appear to be an agency recommendation, promise a successful appeal or imply that a purchase is required to receive service.

Do not ask a worker, beneficiary or service recipient to buy, promote or accept a linked product as part of a home-care visit. Commercial recommendations remain separate from plan decisions, home access, service delivery and complaint handling.
